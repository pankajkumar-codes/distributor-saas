**Material objections (2)**

1. **Major: the accuracy test is not independent (section 10, "Return accuracy" row and 10a week 6 to 22).**
   - The metric says "the CA files independently" and measures "0 differences" against what the CA files.
   - The pilot safety rule and the 10a pilot row, however, have the reviewing CA review each Kartaa return before the seller relies on it.
   - A CA who has seen Kartaa's figures before filing will anchor on them. Zero differences then measures agreement, not accuracy. This is also the gate that decides trust and public launch.
   - Change: say the accuracy sample is prepared blind. For the at least 3 sellers and 3 periods in the test, the CA (or the seller's own CA, via the normal method) prepares and files from the source documents before seeing Kartaa's output. The diff happens afterwards. Reviewing a Kartaa return for reliance applies only to non-sample sellers or periods. A seller who files by their normal method with Kartaa in parallel is the cleanest sample.
   - Also change "the named CA" in the metric row and the Gate B stop rule ("the named CA finds systematic errors") to "reviewing CA", to match section 1.

2. **Major: Gate B's real-payment test has no consequence if it fails while L-5 holds (section 10, Gate B).**
   - The text says "at least 3 of 10 must make a real first payment". The stop list does not include it.
   - The only fallback is "payment unproven" when L-5 does not hold. If L-5 holds and fewer than 3 pay, nothing happens.
   - Change: if L-5 holds and fewer than 3 of 10 pay at the tested price, treat it as a Gate B stop or reposition item. Alternatively, say it records "payment unproven" and caps L-6 at the 20-seller paid soft launch. Pick one.
